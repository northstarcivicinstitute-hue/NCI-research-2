Civic Governance Atlas

Map authority before choosing a course of action.

The Atlas is NCI’s public governance framework for systems where authority, technology, vendors, data and institutional rules overlap. It maps purpose, decision rights, evidence, implementation, accountability and exit before treating a policy or technology as a solution.

What the Atlas doesMaps who decides, who pays, what evidence counts, who controls data and how a system can be challenged or exited.
What it is notA score that declares a technology or policy inherently good or bad.
Primary usePublic-sector decisions where authority and accountability are split across institutions.

AI & Automated Systems Governance

Nine questions before deployment becomes routine.

NCI separates innovation policy, institutional deployment rules and infrastructure governance. The public question is not whether a system is simply “AI”; it is how authority, data and accountability change when the system is used.

PurposeWhat public problem is the system meant to solve?
AuthorityWho can authorize, limit or stop its use?
ProcurementWhat evidence, contract terms and vendor conditions are required?
DataWhat data enter the system, who controls them and where do they go?
DecisionIs the system advisory, or can it materially affect rights, benefits, discipline or access?
Human controlWho can override, reverse or halt an automated outcome?
EvaluationWhat must be tested before and after deployment?
AccountabilityWho receives complaints, audits performance and answers for failure?
ExitHow are contracts ended, data deleted, vendors changed and service continued?
Public-sector AI procurement module

NCI is developing a procurement checklist that converts these nine dimensions into pre-solicitation questions, contract requirements, evaluation criteria, human-oversight rules and exit provisions for municipalities, schools and public agencies.

Michigan Data Center Governance Scorecard

Treat data centers as infrastructure governance.

The scorecard in development will examine who decides, who pays, what must be disclosed and how communities or regulators can review outcomes after a project is approved.

Authority and siting

State, utility and local decision rights; land-use authority; permits; incentives; and the points where public input or appeal exists.

Infrastructure and cost

Generation, transmission, distribution, water and other infrastructure requirements, including which parties bear incremental costs.

Public claims and disclosure

Jobs, tax revenue, economic-development promises, resource use, timelines and the information needed to test those claims later.

Review and exit

Performance reporting, enforcement, changed conditions, closure obligations, transition risk and what happens if expected benefits do not materialize.

Surveillance Governance Series

One governance method across changing surveillance tools.

The series expands NCI’s existing ALPR and gunshot-detection work into a coherent research franchise: license-plate readers, facial recognition, acoustic gunshot detection, drones, commercial/location data, connected vehicles and integrated vendor platforms.

Need and authority

Define the problem, legal authority, permitted uses and the decisions the system can support.

Data and supplier control

Map collection, retention, sharing, cybersecurity, subcontractors, model changes and access across jurisdictions.

Evaluation and exit

Measure operational and public outcomes, not only technical accuracy, and define renewal, termination, deletion and transition rules.

Practitioner review

NCI is comparing how Michigan communities govern license-plate readers after adoption: who authorizes them, how retention and data sharing are set, and who can suspend or end a program. Before publication, NCI is asking city managers, police chiefs and sheriffs to check the field definitions against how the work is actually done. The request is for corrections, not endorsement.

Monitoring file

Election administration and public access.

NCI is monitoring election administration as a governance problem involving overlapping federal and state authority, operational capacity, civil liberties and institutional trust. Any public product will focus on who controls which decisions and how implementation works rather than campaign advocacy.

Research-first posture

NCI can explain policy options, implementation consequences, authority and evidence without telling readers how to vote or presenting contested claims as settled facts. Design options are tied to stated objectives and labeled as NCI analysis.
