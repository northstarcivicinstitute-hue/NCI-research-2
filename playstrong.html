Youth-sports implementation model

PlayStrong

A youth-sports model for connecting young people and families to earlier support, opportunity and referral pathways through institutions they already use.

Discuss a partnershipRead related research

The premise

Youth sports can be a practical access point.

Schools, coaches, leagues and families already form trusted networks around young people. PlayStrong asks how those networks can support earlier information, referral and opportunity without turning coaches into clinicians or creating a separate bureaucracy.

Important boundary

PlayStrong is an implementation model, not a clinical service. Licensed professionals and existing institutional protocols remain responsible for diagnosis, treatment and crisis response.

Core components

What a local implementation can include

Training and literacy

Age-appropriate information for coaches, families and youth-serving adults about warning signs, boundaries and available support.

Referral pathways

Clear routes into existing school, health and community resources rather than informal or improvised advice.

Family communication

Plain-language materials that explain what support exists, who controls decisions and where confidentiality limits apply.

Evaluation

Measures of reach, referral usability, implementation burden and whether the model improves access without creating avoidable risk.

Potential partners

Built for shared implementation.

Potential partners include districts, youth-sport organizations, athletic associations, health systems, community providers, grantmaking partners and evaluators. Roles should be written before launch so coaches and volunteers are not asked to perform clinical or investigative functions.

Start a conversation

Test the model at a manageable scale.

A first partnership should define the population, referral network, training burden, privacy rules, evaluation plan and decision point before implementation.
