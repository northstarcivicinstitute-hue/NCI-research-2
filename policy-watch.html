Sports Policy & Athlete MobilitySept. 24

Senate sets a Monday passage vote on the federal college-sports bill

What happened. The Senate adopted the Cruz-Cantwell substitute to S.4668 by a 77–23 vote and then invoked cloture on the amended bill. A vote on passage is scheduled for about 5:30 p.m. on Monday, September 28.

Why it matters. The bill would move decisions on transfers, eligibility, NIL and athlete representation into a federal framework. Any Senate-passed version would still need House action before it could become law.

Sources: Senate roll call vote 242 · Senate schedule for September 28

Civic & Technology GovernanceThis week

License-plate readers have become a statewide campaign issue

What happened. Bridge Michigan reports that candidates in both parties have addressed Flock license-plate reader cameras, with proposals centered on statewide rules for access, sharing and data retention. In Congress, H.R. 10366, introduced September 14, would condition surface-transportation funding on state limits on license-plate reader use.

Why it matters. The questions candidates are raising, including who can see the data, how long it is kept and who it is shared with, are the same fields NCI is documenting across Michigan communities.

Sources: Bridge Michigan · H.R. 10366 on GovInfo

Civic & Technology GovernanceSept. 25

FTC comment period on personalized pricing closes

What happened. The extended public comment period on the Federal Trade Commission’s proposed enforcement policy statement on personalized pricing closed September 25.

Why it matters. The federal enforcement posture will shape what Michigan’s pending pricing bills need to cover, especially how personal or inferred data may be used to set individual prices.

Sources: FTC comment extension · Proposed policy statement

Civic & Technology GovernanceSept. 16

House passes the data-center ratepayer bill, 417–3

What happened. H.R. 9340 would require state utility regulators to consider rates that make large data-center customers, 100 megawatts or more, pay the full incremental cost of grid upgrades needed to serve them. The bill awaits Senate action.

Why it matters. Michigan communities weighing large data-center projects face exactly this cost-allocation question. If enacted, state regulators would decide how the standard applies here.

Sources: House roll call 312 · CBO cost estimate

Related NCI work: Michigan Data Center Governance Scorecard
Sports Policy & Athlete MobilityOngoing

Court keeps Kalshi sports contracts blocked in Michigan

What happened. On September 1, an Ingham County judge granted the Attorney General a preliminary injunction barring Kalshi from offering sports event contracts in Michigan and requiring geolocation blocking while the case continues.

Why it matters. The case turns on whether sports event contracts are regulated as gambling. That determines which consumer protections, including age limits, apply to products young people can see and use.

Sources: Michigan Attorney General
