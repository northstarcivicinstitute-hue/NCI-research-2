Civic & Technology GovernanceSept. 30

Senate cloture vote on large-load cost legislation fails, 57-43

Fact. The Senate rejected cloture on the motion to proceed to H.R. 9340, the Ratepayer Protection Act, 57-43 on September 30. The measure did not advance through that procedural vote and is not enacted law.

NCI analysis. The vote does not resolve Michigan's underlying data-center governance questions. Cost allocation, utility upgrades, disclosure and local/state authority remain active issues for the Atlas scorecard.

Sports Policy & Athlete MobilitySept. 28

Senate passes federal college-sports bill

What happened. On September 28, 2026, the U.S. Senate passed S.4668, the Protect College Sports Act, 77–22.

Why it matters. The bill now moves to the House. It is not law, and House action could change the final federal framework.

Updated September 30, 2026.

Mobility & PathwaysSept. 30

Michigan College Month puts the application and FAFSA handoff in focus

Fact. MiLEAP is using October's Michigan College Month to encourage high-school seniors to complete college applications and FAFSA steps, with state financial-aid and credential pathways tied to completion.

NCI analysis. This is a measurable handoff problem: opportunity can exist on paper while students still fail to complete the administrative step that unlocks aid or enrollment.

Community Equity Lab →MiLEAP source →
Mobility & PathwaysSept. 25

LEO funds 10 Community Growth Academy implementation projects

Fact. Michigan awarded $1 million across 10 communities in the 18-month Community Growth Academy, which combines technical assistance, peer learning, a full-time fellow and project funding. A second cohort is planned for 2027.

NCI analysis. The coalition-and-handoff model is worth studying as GrowReady develops its own place-based proof of concept; it is a model to learn from, not a program NCI should copy wholesale.

GrowReady →Michigan LEO source →
Civic & Technology GovernanceSept. 24

Michigan joins multi-state call for federal AI standards while preserving state authority

Fact. Attorney General Dana Nessel joined a 26-attorney-general coalition asking Congress for federal AI safety testing and standards, government-led incident response and continued state enforcement authority. The letter is an advocacy request, not enacted law.

NCI analysis. It is a clean Atlas example of federal/state authority, testing, incident response and enforcement being negotiated at the same time.

Atlas AI module →Michigan AG source →
Sports Policy & Athlete MobilityOpens Oct. 1

MGCB opens another $100,000 booster-club funding pool

Fact. MGCB says a new $100,000 pool opens October 1 for eligible school-affiliated booster clubs, with participating organizations able to earn up to $4,000 for approved youth-gambling education.

NCI analysis. The strongest Michigan Sports Opportunity role is implementation learning, education design or independent measurement around an eligible local partner—not presenting the pool as an NCI grant.

Vendor governanceSept. 29

Labcorp settlement puts vendor-control terms in concrete form

Fact. A multistate settlement announced by the Michigan Attorney General requires measures including vendor-risk controls, contract inventories, cybersecurity terms, assessments/audits and termination rights after a third-party data-breach investigation.

NCI analysis. The matter involves private-sector health data, not public procurement, but the controls are a useful comparison set for NCI's supplier-risk and exit questions.

Surveillance governanceData as of Sept. 29

Project Green Light dataset provides a current baseline for a Detroit case study

Fact. As of September 29, Detroit's authoritative Project Green Light locations dataset listed 1,134 participating-location records. The city refreshes the dataset regularly, so the count can change.

NCI analysis. The dataset can anchor the descriptive baseline; the governance case still needs contracts, policies, retention/access rules, public reporting and outcome evidence before NCI draws conclusions.
